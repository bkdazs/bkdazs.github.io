## Introduction: The Heart of Corporate Finance

"Cash is king, but treasury management decides how long the king stays in power."

Treasury management is the heartbeat of corporate finance. It ensures that a company has the right amount of cash, at the right place, at the right time. Poor treasury management has bankrupted profitable companies, while excellent treasury practices have helped struggling companies survive.

---

## What Is Treasury Management?

### Definition

Treasury management is the administration of an organization's holdings—managing liquidity, making decisions about short-term investments, hedging currency and interest rate exposures, and managing banking relationships.

### Core Functions

1. **Cash Management:** Day-to-day liquidity
2. **Liquidity Planning:** Short and medium-term forecasting
3. **Funding Management:** Short-term borrowing and investing
4. **Risk Management:** Currency, interest rate, commodity risks
5. **Banking Relationships:** Account management, services

### Treasury vs Finance

| Treasury | Finance |
|----------|---------|
| Short-term focus | Long-term focus |
| Cash and liquidity | Capital structure |
| Daily operations | Strategic decisions |
| Risk hedging | Risk assessment |
| Banking relationships | Investor relations |

## Cash Management

### Cash Position Management

**Daily Activities:**
1. Check opening bank balances
2. Review expected receipts
3. Review expected payments
4. Calculate net position
5. Invest surplus or arrange borrowing

### Cash Flow Forecasting

**Time Horizons:**

| Horizon | Purpose | Accuracy |
|---------|---------|----------|
| Daily (1-7 days) | Payment planning | Very high |
| Weekly (1-4 weeks) | Liquidity management | High |
| Monthly (1-6 months) | Funding planning | Medium |
| Annual (1 year+) | Strategic planning | Lower |

**Daily Cash Flow Format:**

| Day | Opening | Receipts | Payments | Closing |
|-----|---------|----------|----------|---------|
| Mon | 50 | 100 | (80) | 70 |
| Tue | 70 | 60 | (120) | 10 |
| Wed | 10 | 150 | (50) | 110 |
| Thu | 110 | 80 | (90) | 100 |
| Fri | 100 | 70 | (100) | 70 |

### Collection Optimization

**Accelerating Receivables:**

1. **Electronic collections:**
   - NEFT/RTGS/IMPS
   - UPI for retail
   - Virtual account numbers

2. **Collection float reduction:**
   - Regional collection centers
   - Same-day value dating
   - Auto-debit arrangements

3. **Process improvements:**
   - Prompt invoicing
   - Clear payment terms
   - Dunning procedures

### Payment Optimization

**Managing Payables:**

1. **Payment timing:**
   - Use full credit period
   - Evaluate early payment discounts
   - Centralized payment runs

2. **Payment methods:**
   - Electronic payments (cheaper)
   - Cheques (floating benefit)
   - Letter of credit (supplier comfort)

3. **Payment scheduling:**
   - Batch processing
   - Regular payment days
   - Emergency payment protocols

### Cash Pooling

**What Is Cash Pooling?**
Consolidating cash balances from multiple accounts to optimize interest and simplify management.

**Types:**

**1. Physical Pooling (Zero Balancing):**
- Actual movement of funds
- Subsidiary accounts swept to master
- Interest calculated on consolidated balance

**2. Notional Pooling:**
- No physical movement
- Interest calculated on net position
- Individual accounts maintained

**Benefits:**
- Reduced borrowing
- Better investment returns
- Simplified management
- Lower bank charges

**Scenarios to Test:**

| Scenario | Impact |
|----------|--------|

| Credit line unavailable | -₹100 Cr |

**Survival Period:**
$$Days\ Survived = \frac{Available\ Liquidity}{Daily\ Cash\ Outflow}$$

## Short-Term Investments

### Investment Policy Framework

**Objectives (in order):**
1. **Safety:** Principal protection
2. **Liquidity:** Access when needed
3. **Yield:** Earn reasonable return

### Investment Options (India)

| Instrument | Safety | Liquidity | Typical Yield |
|------------|--------|-----------|---------------|
| Bank FDs | High | Medium | 6-7% |
| Liquid Funds | High | High | 6-7% |
| Treasury Bills | Highest | High | 6-7% |
| Commercial Paper | Medium | Low | 7-8% |
| Overnight Funds | Highest | Highest | 5-6% |

### Investment Decision Criteria

**1. Credit Quality:**
- Bank rating
- Mutual fund AMC reputation
- CP issuer rating

**2. Tenor:**
- Match with expected need date
- Consider reinvestment risk

**3. Amount Limits:**
- Diversification across instruments
- Counterparty limits
- Regulatory limits

### Sample Investment Policy

**Permitted Investments:**
- Bank FDs (AA- rated or above)
- Liquid/money market mutual funds (SEBI regulated)
- Government securities
- Commercial Paper (A1+ rated)

**Limits:**
- Single counterparty: 20% of portfolio
- Single instrument type: 50% of portfolio
- Maximum tenor: 90 days

## Treasury Risk Management

### Types of Treasury Risks

**1. Liquidity Risk:**
Inability to meet obligations when due

**2. Interest Rate Risk:**
Impact of rate changes on costs/returns

**3. Foreign Exchange Risk:**
Currency fluctuation impact

**4. Counterparty Risk:**
Bank or investment failure

**5. Operational Risk:**
Fraud, errors, system failures

### Liquidity Risk Management

**Strategies:**
- Maintain adequate cash buffers
- Diversify funding sources
- Maintain committed credit lines
- Regular stress testing

### Interest Rate Risk Management

**Exposures:**
- Floating rate borrowings
- Investment portfolio duration
- Refinancing risk

**Hedging Tools:**
- Interest rate swaps
- Forward rate agreements
- Interest rate caps/floors

**Example:**
Company with ₹100 crore floating rate loan.
Interest rate rises 1%: Additional cost ₹1 crore/year.
**Hedge:** Enter interest rate swap to fix rate.

### FX Risk Management

**Exposures:**
- Export receivables
- Import payables
- Foreign currency loans
- Overseas investments

**Hedging Tools (India):**
- Forward contracts
- Options (call/put)
- Currency swaps

**Example:**
Export receivable: $1 million due in 90 days
Spot rate: ₹83
Forward rate: ₹83.50
**Action:** Book forward to lock ₹83.50

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What's the ideal cash balance?**
A: Depends on business volatility, payment patterns, and risk tolerance. Generally, 1-3 months of operating expenses as buffer, plus working capital needs.

**Q: Should we hedge all FX exposure?**
A: Not necessarily. Consider materiality, cost of hedging, natural hedges, and company risk policy. Full hedging may not be optimal.

**Q: Bank FD or liquid fund for short-term surplus?**
A: Liquid funds offer better post-tax returns, instant liquidity, and professional management. FDs offer simplicity and relationship benefits. Often use both.

**Q: How many banks should a company work with?**
A: Typically 3-5 relationship banks for diversification, plus specialized banks for specific needs. Too many increases complexity; too few increases risk.

**Q: How to handle cash in multiple currencies?**
A: Multi-currency accounts, regular repatriation/deployment decisions, and hedging strategy for net exposures. Technology helps track positions across currencies.

*Treasury management is like managing your personal finances—but with more zeros, more complexity, and more stakeholders. The principles remain the same: know your cash position, plan ahead, don't take unnecessary risks, and build relationships with your financial partners.*
