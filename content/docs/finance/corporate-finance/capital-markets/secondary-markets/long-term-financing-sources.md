## Introduction: Fueling Business Growth

Every growing business needs capital—for new factories, equipment, technology, expansion, or acquisitions. Where does this money come from?

Long-term financing—funds raised for periods exceeding one year—forms the foundation of a company's capital structure. The choice of financing source affects cost, risk, control, and flexibility for years to come.

This guide covers all major sources of long-term financing available to Indian businesses.

## Equity Financing

### Ordinary Shares (Equity Shares)

**What:** Ownership stake in the company
**Features:**
- Voting rights (control)
- Residual claim on profits (dividends)
- Permanent capital (no maturity)

**Advantages:**
- No fixed repayment obligation
- No interest payments
- Enhances borrowing capacity
- Share risk with investors

**Disadvantages:**
- Most expensive source (highest expected return)
- Dilution of ownership and control
- Dividend expectations (though not mandatory)
- Lengthy and expensive issuance process

### Methods of Equity Issuance

**1. Initial Public Offering (IPO)**
- First-time listing on stock exchange
- Complex regulatory process (SEBI approval)
- Book building or fixed price
- Recent examples: Zomato, Nykaa, LIC

**2. Follow-on Public Offer (FPO)**
- Additional equity by listed company
- Easier than IPO (already listed)
- Example: Yes Bank's FPO

**3. Rights Issue**
- Offer to existing shareholders
- Proportional to current holding
- Usually at discount to market price
- Example: Reliance Rights Issue 2020 (₹53,125 crore)

**4. Private Placement**
- Issue to select investors
- Faster, less regulatory burden
- Qualified Institutional Placement (QIP) for listed companies
- Example: Many IT companies use QIP

**5. Preferential Allotment**
- Issue to specific investors at preferential price
- Often used for promoter infusion
- SEBI regulations on pricing

### Preference Shares

**What:** Hybrid between debt and equity
**Features:**
- Fixed dividend rate
- Priority over equity for dividends
- Priority in liquidation (before equity)
- Usually no voting rights

**Types:**
- Cumulative vs Non-cumulative
- Participating vs Non-participating
- Convertible vs Non-convertible
- Redeemable vs Irredeemable

**In India:**
- Only redeemable preference shares allowed (max 20 years)
- Less popular due to tax treatment (dividends not tax-deductible)

### Retained Earnings

**What:** Profits kept in business instead of distributed as dividends

**Advantages:**
- No issuance costs
- No dilution
- No repayment obligation
- Immediate availability
- No external approvals needed

**Disadvantages:**
- Limited by profitability
- Shareholders may prefer dividends
- May not be sufficient for large projects

**Cost:** Opportunity cost—what shareholders could earn if dividends were paid

## Debt Financing

### Term Loans

**What:** Long-term borrowing from banks/financial institutions

**Features:**
- Fixed or floating interest rate
- Regular EMI payments
- Collateral usually required
- Covenants and conditions

**Types:**

| Type | Tenure | Purpose | Provider |
|------|--------|---------|----------|
| Short-term | Up to 1 year | Working capital | Banks |
| Medium-term | 1-5 years | Equipment, vehicles | Banks, NBFCs |
| Long-term | 5-15+ years | Projects, expansion | Banks, DFIs |

**Major Lenders in India:**
- Commercial Banks (SBI, HDFC, ICICI)
- Development Finance Institutions (IDBI, IFCI, SIDBI)
- NBFCs (L&T Finance, Bajaj Finance)

**Interest Rate Benchmarks:**
- Earlier: MCLR (Marginal Cost of Funds Lending Rate)
- Now: External Benchmark (Repo Rate linked)
- Current range: 8-12% depending on credit quality

### Debentures and Bonds

**What:** Long-term debt securities issued to public or private investors

**Difference:** In practice, used interchangeably in India. Technically, bonds are usually secured, debentures may be secured or unsecured.

**Types of Debentures:**

| Type | Feature |
|------|---------|
| Secured | Backed by collateral |
| Unsecured | No collateral (higher rate) |
| Convertible | Can convert to equity |
| Non-Convertible (NCD) | Pure debt, no conversion |
| Redeemable | Has maturity date |
| Irredeemable | Perpetual (rare) |

**NCD Features (Most Common in India):**
- Fixed or floating coupon
- Tenure: 2-10 years typically
- Rated by credit agencies (CRISIL, ICRA, CARE)
- Listed on exchanges for liquidity

**Example:**
HDFC Ltd NCDs:
- Coupon: 7.5%
- Tenure: 3 years
- Rating: AAA
- Listed on BSE/NSE

### External Commercial Borrowings (ECB)

**What:** Borrowing from foreign lenders in foreign currency

**Advantages:**
- Lower interest rates (global rates)
- Access to larger pools of capital
- Longer tenures available

**Disadvantages:**
- Currency risk (rupee depreciation)
- Hedging costs
- RBI regulations and approvals
- Reporting requirements

**ECB Guidelines (Key Points):**
- Minimum Average Maturity Period (MAMP): 3-5 years
- End-use restrictions (capital expenditure, refinancing)
- All-in-cost ceiling (benchmarked to LIBOR/SOFR)
- Hedging requirements for certain borrowers

**Example:**
Reliance Industries ECB of $2 billion for refinancing, 5-year tenure, LIBOR + spread.

### Public Deposits

**What:** Fixed deposits accepted by companies from public

**Features:**
- Tenure: 6 months to 3 years
- Interest: Usually higher than bank FDs
- Credit risk on depositors
- Regulated by Companies Act

**Limitations:**
- Only eligible companies can accept
- Limits on amount (% of net worth)
- Reserve requirements
- Declining popularity due to past defaults

## Hybrid and Alternative Sources

### Convertible Securities

**Convertible Debentures:**
- Start as debt
- Can convert to equity at predetermined rate
- Lower coupon than straight debt
- Appeal: Debt safety + equity upside

**Foreign Currency Convertible Bonds (FCCBs):**
- Issued in foreign currency
- Convertible to equity
- Popular for raising foreign capital
- Risk: If share price falls, conversion doesn't happen, company must repay foreign currency debt

**Example:** Tata Motors FCCBs—converted when share price rose, diluted equity.

**Optionally Convertible Debentures (OCDs):**
- Holder has option to convert or not
- Flexibility for investor

### Venture Capital and Private Equity

**Venture Capital (VC):**
- For early-stage companies
- High risk, high return expectation
- Active involvement in management
- Exit through IPO or sale

**Private Equity (PE):**
- For mature companies
- Larger deal sizes
- May take controlling stake
- Operational improvements focus

**Major Players in India:**
- VC: Sequoia, Accel, Tiger Global, Peak XV Partners
- PE: Blackstone, KKR, Carlyle, Warburg Pincus, BPEA

**PE/VC Investment Stages:**
- Seed/Angel: ₹25 lakh - ₹2 crore
- Series A: ₹5-25 crore
- Series B/C: ₹25-100 crore
- Growth PE: ₹100+ crore
- Buyout: Large controlling stakes

### Lease Financing

**What:** Using an asset without owning it

**Operating Lease:**
- Short-term, cancellable
- No ownership transfer
- Off-balance sheet (under old accounting)
- Example: Office space, equipment

**Finance Lease (Capital Lease):**
- Long-term, non-cancellable
- Ownership may transfer at end
- Asset and liability on balance sheet
- Example: Aircraft, heavy machinery

**Advantages:**
- 100% financing (no down payment)
- Preserves working capital
- Tax benefits (lease payments deductible)
- Off-balance sheet (operating lease, pre-Ind AS 116)

**Major Lessors in India:**
- Srei Equipment Finance
- Tata Capital
- L&T Finance
- Bank-affiliated NBFCs

### Asset Securitization

**What:** Converting illiquid assets into tradeable securities

**Process:**
1. Originator pools assets (loans, receivables)
2. Sells to Special Purpose Vehicle (SPV)
3. SPV issues securities backed by asset pool
4. Investors buy securities, receive cash flows from assets

**Examples in India:**
- Mortgage-backed securities (housing loans)
- Auto loan securitization
- Microfinance receivables

**Benefits:**
- Off-balance sheet financing
- Risk transfer
- Improved liquidity ratios
- Access to capital markets

## Choosing the Right Source

### Key Considerations

| Factor | Equity Preferred | Debt Preferred |
|--------|-----------------|----------------|
| Cost | Can afford higher cost | Need lower cost |
| Control | Willing to share | Want to retain |
| Risk | High business risk | Stable cash flows |
| Flexibility | Need flexibility | Can commit to payments |
| Tax | Already in low bracket | Can use tax shields |
| Collateral | Limited assets | Strong collateral |
| Growth | High growth needs | Moderate, stable growth |

### Decision Framework

**Question 1:** What is the purpose?
- Growth/expansion: Equity or long-term debt
- Equipment: Lease or term loan
- Refinancing: Cheaper source available?

**Question 2:** What is company's risk profile?
- High business risk: Less debt
- Stable business: Can use more debt

**Question 3:** What is the cost comparison?
- Calculate effective cost of each source
- Consider tax implications

**Question 4:** What are the control implications?
- Promoters wanting control: Prefer debt
- Open to dilution: Equity acceptable

**Question 5:** What is market condition?
- Bull market: Equity favorable
- Low interest rates: Debt favorable

## Financing Sources by Company Stage

### Startup/Early Stage

**Typical Sources:**
- Founder savings
- Friends and family
- Angel investors
- Seed/Series A VC

**Debt Options:** Very limited (no collateral, no track record)

### Growth Stage

**Typical Sources:**
- Series B/C VC
- Growth PE
- Venture debt
- Bank debt (if profitable)

**Key Need:** Capital for rapid scaling

### Mature Stage

**Typical Sources:**
- Term loans
- NCDs/bonds
- Public equity (FPO, rights)
- Retained earnings

**Key Need:** Optimization of capital structure

### Turnaround/Distress

**Typical Sources:**
- Distressed debt funds
- Restructuring
- Asset sales
- Rights issue (heavily discounted)

**Key Need:** Survival, debt reduction

## Regulatory Framework in India

### SEBI Regulations

- IPO/FPO: SEBI (Issue of Capital and Disclosure Requirements)
- Private Placement: Companies Act + SEBI
- NCDs: SEBI (Issue and Listing of Non-Convertible Securities)

### RBI Regulations

- ECBs: Master Direction on ECB
- Bank lending: Various guidelines
- NBFCs: NBFC regulations

### Companies Act 2013

- Private placement limits
- Debenture redemption reserve
- Public deposit acceptance rules
- Inter-corporate loans and investments

## Key Takeaways

1. **Equity is permanent but expensive** – No repayment, but high return expectations
2. **Debt is cheaper but risky** – Tax shields but fixed obligations
3. **Retained earnings are best** – No cost, no dilution, but limited
4. **Hybrids offer flexibility** – Convertibles combine features
5. **PE/VC for high-growth companies** – Patient capital with involvement
6. **Lease for asset-heavy needs** – Preserve capital, get flexibility
7. **Match source to purpose** – Each source has ideal use case

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: What is the cheapest source of long-term finance?**
A: Generally retained earnings (no explicit cost), then debt (tax-deductible), then equity (highest expected return).

**Q: Why don't companies use only debt?**
A: Risk of bankruptcy. At some point, debt service becomes unsustainable and distress costs outweigh tax benefits.

**Q: How do startups raise money without revenue?**
A: Equity from angels and VCs who invest based on potential, not current performance. Debt is rarely available to pre-revenue companies.

**Q: What is the difference between NCD and bond?**
A: In India, largely interchangeable terms. Technically, debentures are unsecured debt, bonds are secured. But usage has blurred this distinction.

**Q: Is lease financing better than loan?**
A: Depends on situation. Lease preserves capital and may have tax benefits, but may cost more overall. Analyze specific terms.

*Long-term financing is like building the foundation of a house. Choose the wrong materials, and the structure becomes unstable. Choose wisely—considering cost, risk, control, and flexibility—and your business has a solid base for growth.*
