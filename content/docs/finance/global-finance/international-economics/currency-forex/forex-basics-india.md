## Introduction: The World's Largest Market

"The foreign exchange market trades over $7 trillion daily—more than all stock markets combined. Understanding forex is essential in our interconnected global economy."

Whether you're an investor, importer, traveler, or simply tracking your mutual fund's international exposure, foreign exchange affects your life. Understanding how currencies work helps you make better financial decisions and comprehend global economic events.

## What is Foreign Exchange?

### Definition

Foreign exchange (forex or FX) is the conversion of one currency into another. The forex market is where currencies are traded.

### Market Size

| Metric | Value |
|--------|-------|
| Daily Turnover | $7.5+ trillion |
| Market Type | Over-the-counter (OTC) |
| Trading Hours | 24 hours, 5.5 days |
| Major Centers | London, New York, Tokyo, Singapore |

### Why Forex Exists

**Needs Driving Forex:**
| Need | Example |
|------|---------|
| International trade | Importer paying for goods |
| Investment flows | FPI buying Indian stocks |
| Travel/Tourism | Buying dollars for US trip |
| Remittances | NRI sending money home |
| Speculation | Trading currency movements |
| Hedging | Protecting export revenues |

## Currency Pairs

### Understanding Pairs

Currencies are always quoted in pairs—one against another.

**Format:** BASE/QUOTE (or BASE QUOTE)

**Example: USD/INR**
- Base currency: USD (US Dollar)
- Quote currency: INR (Indian Rupee)
- Rate 83.00 means: 1 USD = 83 INR

### Major Currency Pairs

| Pair | Name | Description |
|------|------|-------------|
| EUR/USD | Euro/Dollar | Most traded pair |
| USD/JPY | Dollar/Yen | Second most traded |
| GBP/USD | Pound/Dollar | "Cable" |
| USD/CHF | Dollar/Franc | Safe haven pair |

### INR Pairs

| Pair | Trading |
|------|---------|
| USD/INR | Most liquid INR pair |
| EUR/INR | Second most traded |
| GBP/INR | Active |
| JPY/INR | Less liquid |

### Quote Convention

**Direct Quote (India):**
- Foreign currency per unit of domestic
- USD/INR = 83 (1 USD = 83 INR)

**Indirect Quote:**
- Domestic currency per unit of foreign
- INR/USD = 0.012 (1 INR = 0.012 USD)

## Exchange Rate Basics

### Bid and Ask

| Term | Definition | Example (USD/INR) |
|------|------------|-------------------|
| Bid | Bank buys USD from you | 82.90 |
| Ask | Bank sells USD to you | 83.10 |
| Spread | Difference | 0.20 (20 paise) |

**Remember:**
- You sell at bid (lower)
- You buy at ask (higher)
- Bank always wins the spread

### Pips and Points

**Pip:** Smallest price increment

| Pair | Pip Size |
|------|----------|
| USD/INR | 0.0025 (quarter paisa) |
| EUR/USD | 0.0001 (4th decimal) |
| USD/JPY | 0.01 (2nd decimal) |

### Appreciation vs Depreciation

**Rupee Strengthens:**
- USD/INR falls (83 → 82)
- Same rupees buy more dollars
- Good for importers, foreign travel

**Rupee Weakens:**
- USD/INR rises (83 → 84)
- Need more rupees for same dollars
- Good for exporters, IT companies

## Exchange Rate Determination

### Factors Affecting Rates

| Factor | Impact on INR |
|--------|--------------|
| Interest rate differential | Higher India rates → INR appreciates |
| Inflation differential | Higher India inflation → INR depreciates |
| Trade balance | Trade deficit → INR pressure |
| Capital flows | FPI inflows → INR appreciates |
| RBI intervention | Smooths volatility |
| Global risk sentiment | Risk-off → INR weakens |
| Oil prices | Higher oil → INR weakens |

### Interest Rate Parity

$$Forward\ Rate = Spot\ Rate \times \frac{(1 + r_{INR})}{(1 + r_{USD})}$$

**Example:**
- Spot USD/INR: 83.00
- India 1-year rate: 7%
- US 1-year rate: 5%
- Forward: 83 × (1.07/1.05) = 84.58

### Purchasing Power Parity (PPP)

**Theory:** Exchange rates adjust for price level differences

**Big Mac Index Example:**
- Big Mac in US: $5.69
- Big Mac in India: ₹200
- Implied rate: 200/5.69 = 35.15
- Actual rate: 83
- INR "undervalued" by PPP

## Forex Market Structure

### Market Participants

| Participant | Role |
|-------------|------|
| Central banks | Policy, intervention |
| Commercial banks | Market makers |
| Corporations | Trade-related needs |
| Investment funds | Portfolio flows |
| Speculators | Profit from movements |

### Interbank Market

**Characteristics:**
- Banks trade with each other
- Wholesale market
- Large transaction sizes ($1M+)
- Tight spreads

### Retail Market

**Characteristics:**
- Banks/dealers to customers
- Wider spreads
- Smaller transaction sizes
- Money changers, travel cards

## Spot and Forward Markets

### Spot Market

**Definition:** Immediate exchange at current rate

**Settlement:**
| Currency | Settlement |
|----------|------------|
| Most pairs | T+2 |
| USD/CAD | T+1 |

**Use Case:**
- Immediate currency needs
- Travel
- Current import payments

### Forward Market

**Definition:** Agreement to exchange at future date at agreed rate

**Features:**
| Aspect | Detail |
|--------|--------|
| Settlement | Future date (1M, 3M, 6M, 1Y) |
| Rate | Fixed at contract time |
| Customizable | Amount, date flexible |
| OTC | Traded between parties |

**Use Case:**
- Hedge future forex exposure
- Lock in rate for expected payment/receipt

### Forward Premium/Discount

**Premium:** Forward > Spot (INR at premium = INR expected to depreciate)
**Discount:** Forward < Spot

**Formula:**
$$Premium = \frac{(Forward - Spot)}{Spot} \times \frac{12}{Months} \times 100\%$$

## Forex and Investments

### Impact on Equity

**IT Companies:**
- Revenue in USD
- Rupee depreciation → Higher INR revenue
- Benefit from weak rupee

**Importers (Oil, Electronics):**
- Pay in USD
- Rupee depreciation → Higher costs
- Hurt by weak rupee

### International Investing

**When You Invest Abroad:**
- Double return/risk: Asset + Currency
- Strong INR = Lower returns in INR terms
- Weak INR = Higher returns in INR terms

**Example:**
| Item | Value |
|------|-------|
| US stock return | +10% |
| USD/INR change | +5% (rupee weakened) |
| Total return in INR | ~+15.5% |

## Frequently Asked Questions

**Q: Where can I check live USD/INR rate?**
A: RBI reference rate published at 1:30 PM daily. Real-time rates on financial websites (Bloomberg, Money Control), bank websites, or broker platforms.

**Q: Why do banks offer different rates?**
A: Banks add margin (spread) to interbank rate. Competition, volume, and customer relationship affect the spread offered.

**Q: Is forex trading legal in India?**
A: Trading INR pairs (USD/INR, EUR/INR, etc.) on recognized exchanges (NSE, BSE) is legal. Cross-currency pairs and overseas forex trading through unauthorized platforms is not allowed.

**Q: How does weak rupee affect my international mutual fund?**
A: If rupee weakens, your international fund returns increase in INR terms (favorable currency translation). If rupee strengthens, returns decrease.

**Q: Why does oil price affect rupee?**
A: India imports ~85% of oil needs. Higher oil prices mean more dollars needed to pay for oil, increasing dollar demand and pressuring rupee.

*The forex market connects the world's economies, and the rupee's movement tells the story of India's economic relationship with the rest of the world. Understanding forex makes you a more informed investor and citizen.*
