# Gold Loans in India: Complete Guide

Gold loans are one of the most accessible and affordable forms of credit in India. By pledging your gold jewelry or coins, you can get quick funds at relatively low interest rates. This guide covers everything you need to know about gold loans.

## What Is a Gold Loan?

### Definition

A gold loan is a secured loan where you pledge gold jewelry or coins as collateral. The lender provides a loan amount based on the gold's value, and you get your gold back when you repay the loan.

### How It Works

```
Step 1: Bring gold to lender
Step 2: Gold evaluated and weighed
Step 3: Loan amount offered (based on value)
Step 4: Documentation and disbursement (often same day)
Step 5: Repay loan per chosen scheme
Step 6: Get gold back after full repayment
```

### Key Features

| Feature | Typical Terms |
|---------|--------------|
| Loan Amount | 75-90% of gold value |
| Interest Rate | 7-15% |
| Tenure | 3-36 months |
| Processing | Same day to 2 days |
| Minimum Gold | 10-20 grams typically |
| Maximum Loan | Up to ₹2-5 crores |

## Loan-to-Value (LTV) Ratio

### RBI Guidelines

**Maximum LTV Allowed:**
```
RBI caps gold loan LTV at:
- Banks: 75% of gold value
- NBFCs: 75% of gold value

Example:
Gold Value: ₹5,00,000
Maximum Loan: ₹3,75,000 (75%)
```

### How Gold Is Valued

```
Valuation Formula:
Loan Amount = Weight × Purity × Gold Rate × LTV%

Example:
Gold Weight: 50 grams
Purity: 22 karat
Current Gold Rate: ₹5,000/gram (for 24k)
22k Value: 50 × (22/24) × 5,000 = ₹2,29,166
Loan at 75% LTV: ₹1,71,875
```

### Factors Affecting Loan Amount

**Gold Purity:**
```
24 Karat: 99.9% pure - Highest value
22 Karat: 91.6% pure - Most jewelry
20 Karat: 83.3% pure - Lower value
18 Karat: 75% pure - Lowest acceptable usually
```

**What's Accepted:**
- Gold jewelry (necklaces, bangles, earrings)
- Gold coins (bank-issued)
- Gold bars (with certification)

**What's NOT Accepted:**
- Gold-plated items
- Less than 18 karat purity
- Ancestral items with embedded gems (sometimes)
- Damaged or very old gold

## Repayment Options

### Option 1: Bullet Repayment

```
How It Works:
- Pay only interest monthly
- Pay entire principal at end
- No EMI burden during tenure

Example:
Loan: ₹2,00,000 at 12%
Tenure: 12 months
Monthly Interest: ₹2,000
Final Payment: ₹2,00,000

Good For: Business owners with variable cash flow
Risk: Must have lump sum at end
```

### Option 2: Regular EMI

```
How It Works:
- Fixed monthly payment
- Includes principal + interest
- Like normal loan

Example:
Loan: ₹2,00,000 at 12%
Tenure: 12 months
EMI: ₹17,769

Good For: Salaried individuals
Benefit: Structured repayment
```

### Option 3: Interest Upfront

```
How It Works:
- Interest deducted from loan at start
- Pay only principal at end

Example:
Loan: ₹2,00,000 at 12%
Tenure: 12 months
Interest Deducted: ₹24,000
You Receive: ₹1,76,000
Pay Back: ₹2,00,000

Good For: Short tenure loans
Note: Effective rate is higher
```

### Option 4: Overdraft (Gold OD)

```
How It Works:
- Credit line against gold
- Withdraw as needed
- Pay interest only on used amount

Example:
Sanctioned: ₹5,00,000
Used: ₹2,00,000
Interest On: ₹2,00,000 only

Good For: Business needs, variable requirements
Benefit: Flexibility, pay for what you use
```

## Risks and Considerations

### Risk 1: Gold Price Fluctuation

```
Scenario:
Loan Taken: Gold value ₹5,00,000, Loan ₹3,75,000
Gold Price Drops: Gold value now ₹4,00,000, Loan still ₹3,75,000

LTV Ratio: Now 94% (exceeds 75%)

Action Required:
- Lender may ask for top-up collateral
- Or partial repayment
- Or face gold sale

Protection:
- Don't take maximum LTV
- Monitor gold prices
- Maintain repayment buffer
```

### Risk 2: Inability to Repay

```
Consequence:
- Lender can auction gold
- Auction after notice period
- You lose gold permanently

Prevention:
- Borrow only what you can repay
- Have repayment plan before borrowing
- Keep emergency buffer
```

### Risk 3: High Interest on NBFCs

```
Some NBFCs charge 15-24%:
- Higher than bank gold loans
- Can become expensive
- Compare before choosing

Always Compare:
- At least 3-4 lenders
- Total cost, not just rate
```

### Risk 4: Renewal Trap

```
Bullet Repayment Pitfall:
Month 1-12: Pay only interest
Month 12: Can't pay principal
Solution?: "Renew" loan

Problem:
- Interest on interest accumulates
- Debt grows
- May eventually lose gold

Prevention:
- Choose EMI scheme
- Save for principal payment
- Don't depend on renewal
```

## Common Mistakes

### Mistake 1: Not Comparing Lenders

```
Difference Can Be Significant:
Lender A: 9% interest
Lender B: 15% interest

On ₹2,00,000 for 1 year:
Cost Difference: ₹12,000

Always get 3-4 quotes.
```

### Mistake 2: Taking Maximum LTV

```
Risk:
Gold Value Drops → Margin Call → Forced Repayment

Better:
Borrow 60-65% of value
Leave safety margin
```

### Mistake 3: Repeated Renewals

```
Trap:
Year 1: Borrow ₹2,00,000
Pay Interest: ₹24,000
Renew

Year 2: Borrow ₹2,00,000
Pay Interest: ₹24,000
Renew

5 Years: Paid ₹1,20,000 interest, still owe ₹2,00,000

Exit Strategy: Plan repayment, not renewals.
```

### Mistake 4: Using for Consumption

```
Not Ideal:
Gold loan for vacation, gadgets, lifestyle

Better:
Gold loan for business, emergency, opportunity

Reason:
You're paying interest to buy something depreciating.
```

## Conclusion

Gold loans are a valuable financial tool when used wisely. They offer quick access to affordable credit without credit checks or extensive documentation. The key is borrowing responsibly and having a clear repayment plan.

**Key Takeaways:**

1. **Compare multiple lenders**—rate differences are significant
2. **Don't max out LTV**—leave safety margin
3. **Have repayment plan**—don't depend on renewals
4. **Choose right scheme**—EMI vs. bullet based on your cash flow
5. **Read all terms**—understand foreclosure and charges
6. **Borrow for productive use**—investment, business, emergency
7. **Get your gold back**—that's the goal

Gold loans should be a bridge to meet temporary needs, not a permanent debt burden. Use them wisely, and they're one of the best credit tools available.
