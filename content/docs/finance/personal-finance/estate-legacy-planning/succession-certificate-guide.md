# Succession Certificate in India

A Succession Certificate is a legal document issued by a court that authorizes the holder to inherit debts and securities of a deceased person. It is essential for transferring certain assets and is more powerful than a Legal Heir Certificate.

## What Is a Succession Certificate?

### Definition

```
Succession Certificate:
A court-issued document under:
- Indian Succession Act, 1925
- Sections 370-390

Authorizes Holder To:
- Receive debts due to deceased
- Transfer securities
- Collect assets from debtors
- Represent estate legally
```

### Scope of Certificate

```
Covers:
- Bank deposits and accounts
- Fixed deposits
- Government securities
- Shares and debentures
- Bonds
- Mutual fund units
- Debts owed to deceased

Does NOT Cover:
- Immovable property
- Physical assets
- Personal belongings
- Vehicles (mostly)
```

### Issued By

```
Competent Court:
- District Court
- Where deceased lived
- Or where property exists

Not Issued By:
- Revenue authorities
- Tehsildar
- SDM
```

## When Is Succession Certificate Required?

### Mandatory Situations

```
Required For:
1. Bank accounts (high balance)
2. Fixed deposits (above limits)
3. Shares transfer
4. Mutual fund units transfer
5. Debentures
6. Government securities
7. NSC, KVP redemption
8. When specifically demanded
```

### Threshold Limits

```
Banks May Accept Without:
- Small balances (< ₹1-2 lakh)
- With indemnity bond
- Joint accounts
- Nomination exists

Banks Require For:
- Higher balances (> ₹2-5 lakh)
- No nomination
- Disputes exist
- Corporate policy
```

### When Not Required

```
Usually Exempt:
- Nominated accounts
- Joint accounts (survivorship)
- Insurance with nominee
- Small amounts
- EPF/PPF with nominee
```

## How Is It Different?

### vs. Legal Heir Certificate

```
Legal Heir Certificate:
- From revenue authority
- Simple process
- Low fees
- Limited acceptance
- For government claims

Succession Certificate:
- From court
- Complex process
- Higher fees
- Universal acceptance
- For securities/debts
```

### vs. Probate

```
Probate:
- When there's a will
- Validates the will
- Court certified will
- Covers all estate

Succession Certificate:
- When no will
- Or will not probated
- Specific to debts/securities
- Under Succession Act
```

### vs. Letters of Administration

```
Letters of Administration:
- When no will exists
- Or executor not appointed
- Broader authority
- Covers entire estate

Succession Certificate:
- More limited scope
- Only debts and securities
- Simpler than LOA
- Specific items listed
```

## Who Can Apply?

### Eligible Applicants

```
Legal Heirs:
- Spouse
- Children
- Parents
- Siblings (if no closer heirs)
- Any person claiming to be heir

Not Limited To:
- Only one heir
- Only eldest
- Only male
```

### Multiple Heirs

```
Options:
1. One heir applies, others consent
2. All heirs apply jointly
3. One contests, court decides

Usually:
- One heir applies with NOC from others
- Certificate names all heirs
- Shares specified
```

## Court Procedure

### Jurisdiction

```
Where to Apply:
- District where deceased ordinarily resided
- Or district where estate (part of) exists

Example:
Deceased lived in Delhi
Bank account in Mumbai

Can apply in Delhi (residence)
```

### Step 1: Draft Petition

```
Petition Should Contain:
□ Court and jurisdiction
□ Deceased's details
□ Date, time, place of death
□ Petitioner's details
□ Relationship with deceased
□ List of all legal heirs
□ Assets for which certificate needed
□ Value of assets
□ Whether will exists
□ Prayer for certificate
```

### Step 2: File Petition

```
At District Court:
- Civil section
- Pay court fees
- Get case number
- Submit documents
```

### Step 3: Publication

```
Court Orders:
- Notice in newspaper
- Official Gazette
- Notice at courthouse
- Served on heirs (if known)

Purpose:
- Inform interested parties
- Allow objections
- 45-day minimum period
```

### Step 4: Hearing

```
After Publication Period:
- Court hearing
- Verify documents
- Hear objections (if any)
- Examine petitioner
- Review evidence
```

### Step 5: Order

```
If Satisfied:
- Court grants certificate
- Lists specific assets
- Names beneficiaries
- Specifies shares

Time:
- 3-6 months (no contest)
- 1-3 years (if contested)
```

## Documents Required

### Essential Documents

```
1. Death Certificate (Original + Copy)

2. Proof of Relationship:
   - Marriage certificate
   - Birth certificates
   - Family tree

3. List of Assets:
   - Bank statements
   - FD receipts
   - Share certificates
   - Demat statement

4. Petitioner's ID Proof:
   - Aadhar, PAN, etc.

5. Address Proof:
   - Of deceased and petitioner

6. Affidavit:
   - Verifying facts
   - On stamp paper
```

### Additional Documents

```
May Be Required:
- NOC from other heirs
- Indemnity bond
- Valuation of assets
- Proof of residence
- Will (if any, to show none)
- Death registration
```

## Court Fees

### Ad Valorem Fees

```
Fees Based on Asset Value:
Court fees = Percentage of asset value

Varies by State:
Typically:
- 2-5% of asset value
- Different slabs
- Maximum caps may apply
```

### Fee Examples

```
Maharashtra:
Assets up to ₹50,000: 1%
₹50,000-1 lakh: ₹500 + 0.5%
Above ₹1 lakh: varies

Delhi:
Approximately 3% of value

Karnataka:
Different slab rates

Check:
Your state's Court Fees Act
```

### Other Costs

```
Additional Expenses:
- Lawyer fees: ₹5,000-50,000+
- Stamp paper for affidavit
- Newspaper publication: ₹2,000-5,000
- Certified copies: ₹50-100 per page
- Miscellaneous: ₹1,000-2,000
```

### Total Cost Example

```
Asset Value: ₹10,00,000

Court fees (@3%): ₹30,000
Lawyer fees: ₹15,000
Publication: ₹3,000
Other costs: ₹2,000
--------------------------
Total: ~₹50,000 (approx.)
```

## Petition Format

### Sample Petition Structure

```
IN THE COURT OF DISTRICT JUDGE, [DISTRICT]

SUCCESSION CASE NO. ___/____

In the matter of:
The Indian Succession Act, 1925

AND

In the matter of:
Estate of Late [Deceased Name]

[Petitioner Name]                    ...Petitioner
Address: [Full Address]

PETITION UNDER SECTION 372 OF THE 
INDIAN SUCCESSION ACT, 1925

1. That the petitioner is the [relationship] 
   of late [Deceased Name].

2. That the deceased expired on [Date] at 
   [Place].

3. That the deceased at the time of death 
   was ordinarily resident at [Address], 
   within the jurisdiction of this Hon'ble 
   Court.

4. That the deceased left behind the 
   following legal heirs:
   a) [Name], [Relation], [Address]
   b) [Name], [Relation], [Address]

5. That the deceased left the following 
   assets:
   [List bank accounts, shares, etc.]

6. That no proceedings for probate or 
   letters of administration have been 
   instituted.

7. That the petitioner requires succession 
   certificate to receive the above assets.

PRAYER:
It is therefore most respectfully prayed 
that this Hon'ble Court may be pleased to:
a) Grant succession certificate in favor 
   of the petitioner
b) [Other reliefs]

PETITIONER

Place: 
Date:

VERIFICATION
[Standard verification clause]
```

## Timeline

### Expected Duration

```
Without Contest:
- Filing to first hearing: 1-2 months
- Publication period: 45+ days
- Total: 3-6 months

With Contest:
- Can extend to 1-3 years
- Depends on nature of contest
- Evidence, hearings
```

### Factors Affecting Time

```
Delays Caused By:
- Incomplete documents
- Objections filed
- Court workload
- Multiple assets/jurisdictions
- Holiday periods
- COVID-related delays
```

## Using the Certificate

### What You Can Do

```
With Certificate:
- Approach bank/institution
- Show certificate
- Complete their formalities
- Receive assets/transfer securities
- Issue receipts on behalf of estate
```

### Indemnity Bond

```
May Still Be Required:
- By banks/institutions
- Additional security
- In case of false claim
- Standard procedure
```

### Certificate Scope

```
Limited To:
- Assets listed in certificate
- Get separate certificate for others
- Can apply to include more assets
```

## Contest and Objections

### Who Can Object

```
Objectors:
- Other legal heirs
- Creditors of deceased
- Anyone with interest
```

### Grounds for Objection

```
Common Objections:
- Wrong relationship claimed
- Heir excluded
- Assets belong to objector
- Deceased had will
- Different jurisdiction
- Fraud alleged
```

### If Contested

```
Procedure:
- Written objection filed
- Evidence presented
- Witnesses examined
- Arguments heard
- Court decides

Impact:
- Significant delays
- Higher costs
- Uncertain outcome
```

## Special Situations

### NRI Applicant

```
NRI Can Apply:
- Through Indian lawyer
- POA holder can represent
- Appear via video (some courts)
- Additional documentation
```

### Multiple States

```
Assets in Different States:
Option 1: Apply where deceased resided
Option 2: Separate certificates per state
Option 3: Transfer to one court

Recommendation:
Apply in residence court, approach 
other institutions with that certificate
```

### Minor Heirs

```
When Heir is Minor:
- Natural guardian applies
- On behalf of minor
- Court may require guardian
- Extra scrutiny
```

## After Receiving Certificate

### Steps to Follow

```
1. Get certified copies (multiple)
2. Approach each institution
3. Submit certificate + their forms
4. Complete KYC requirements
5. Sign indemnity bonds
6. Receive assets/transfers
```

### Bank Process

```
With Certificate:
□ Visit branch
□ Submit certificate (certified copy)
□ Fill bank's forms
□ KYC of claimant
□ Indemnity bond
□ Signatures verified
□ Processing
□ Amount credited/transferred
```

### Shares Transfer

```
Process:
□ Approach registrar
□ Submit certificate
□ Transmission form
□ KYC documents
□ Share transfer executed
□ Name changed in records
```

## Common Mistakes to Avoid

### Errors in Process

```
1. Incomplete Petition:
   - Missing heirs
   - Wrong asset list
   - Incorrect values

2. Wrong Court:
   - Jurisdiction issues
   - Dismissal possible

3. Not Publishing:
   - Objection period required
   - Court won't proceed

4. Not Attending Hearings:
   - Dismissed for default
   - Start over
```

## Conclusion

A Succession Certificate is the definitive document for inheriting debts and securities of a deceased person in India. While more complex and expensive than a Legal Heir Certificate, it is often necessary for significant assets.

**Key Takeaways:**

1. **Court process required**—not administrative
2. **Ad valorem fees**—based on asset value
3. **45-day publication**—mandatory notice period
4. **3-6 months typical**—longer if contested
5. **Specific to assets listed**—not general authority
6. **Hire a lawyer**—complex legal procedure
7. **Get multiple copies**—needed for various institutions
8. **Check if needed**—smaller amounts may not require

**When Required:**
- High-value bank accounts
- Share transfers
- Securities
- When specifically demanded
